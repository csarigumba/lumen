I recently read about someone whose story stuck with me.

This person was always in constant motion. Always doing something, always chasing the next thing, never slowing down to question whether he was living his own story or just running on autopilot. After years of this, he got severely ill and lost almost everything. His career, his achievements, the identity he had built around being productive. He couldn't even do simple things like driving. Everything that defined him was stripped away.

You'd expect that to break someone. But something unexpected happened. Instead of falling into depression, he found clarity. He began a journey of self-discovery. Losing everything forced him to finally sit still and ask the questions he had been avoiding his entire life.

I'm sharing this because his story resonates with me. Lately, I've been feeling something similar. Not the illness, but the tension. I want to do something more with my life, but I'm too afraid to go all in. I wrote about this a bit in my [personal mission statement](/posts/why-you-need-a-personal-mission-statement), the idea of being busy without really knowing why. And in my post about [Meditations](/posts/meditations-by-marcus-aurelius), Marcus Aurelius talks about the same thing: the importance of living intentionally instead of being carried along by routine.

Here are eight lessons I took away from this person's story.

## 1. Always Listen to Your Intuition

If you feel you need to slow down or take a break, listen to your body. It's giving you a signal. So take a rest and resume once you're fully recharged.

Ignoring these signals leads to invasive thoughts and eventually burnout. And if you keep ignoring it, sooner or later, you will pay for how you've been living.

## 2. Always Enjoy the Present Moment

Focus on what is now, not what's next.

We can't enjoy the present moment if we're constantly thinking about the future. What will be our next task? What will be our next goal? The problem with this kind of thinking is that it steals the present moment, which, practically speaking, is the only thing we really own.

We don't own the past. We don't own the future. The past already happened, and the future is not guaranteed. What we really own is this instant moment, these instant seconds. So as much as possible, cherish each present moment.

## 3. Always Practice Gratitude

Most of the time, we're focused on chasing the things we don't have. What's my next goal? What's my next target? I'm not sure if this is just me, but I feel it's cultural. We're conditioned to focus on what we lack.

And because of this, we're constantly chasing. But chasing what's missing won't bring fulfillment. We also don't appreciate what we have until it's gone. For example, we take our parents for granted, not realizing that they're getting older and weaker. We should be spending more time with them while we still can.

This taught me that in order to practice gratitude, it sometimes requires us to slow down, to look around and inside.

## 4. Always See the Best in the People Around You

When we're stressed and tired of life's challenges, tired of conflicts, tired of all the external noise, it's good to pause and realize that you're surrounded by good people.

Even in difficulty, there's much to be grateful for. For me, it's knowing I have a loving and caring wife, supportive parents, and a thoughtful brother. But when you're already deep in stress, it's hard to reflect on this. You have to slow down to see what's already there.

## 5. If You Want to Do Something, Take Action Now

Stop waiting for the right time or the right situation. If you feel something stirring inside you, it's there for a reason.

Of course, it's good to reflect before committing to something. But too much overthinking leads to analysis paralysis. You don't need perfection to start living. Life is short, and I think the reason many of us aren't happy is because we're too afraid to pursue the things we actually want.

## 6. Always Protect Your Time

Our time is our most valuable asset. We can earn money, but we cannot earn time. It's our currency.

What you pay attention to determines how you feel, think, and choose. So in order to have a more meaningful life, always ask yourself: does doing this make me feel better or worse? The answer will redirect your attention toward what builds you up. Away from triggers and negativity, and toward healing and positive possibilities.

## 7. Always Find an Opportunity to Better Yourself

Always be courageous, even in small ways. It doesn't have to be a big leap. Even a small challenge outside your comfort zone is enough. The idea is to constantly expose yourself to growth so you can discover the best version of yourself.

I think the effort itself matters more than the outcome. If we can maintain a growth mindset, it pushes our boundaries, which leads to passion and progress. For example, I'm not naturally a writer. But recently, I've been consistently publishing articles to share my experiences and perspectives. The point isn't whether they go viral. The point is showing up every day, staying consistent, and building.

## 8. Always Tell the People You Care About That You Love Them

We're lucky because we still have the chance. Always tell the people you care about that you appreciate them and that you love them. Be specific about what you appreciate. It'll make both of you feel good, and the more you do it, the better.

## Final Thought

These lessons aren't new. They're the kind of things you read in books and nod along to but rarely internalize. What made this story different for me is that the person didn't choose to learn them. Life forced him to. He had to lose everything before he could see what actually mattered.

I don't want to wait for that. And if you're reading this, maybe you don't either. ✌️
