Most days do not fall apart because of one big problem. They get pulled apart by small things.

A message arrives at the wrong time. A meeting moves slowly. A plan changes. Someone is in a bad mood. I feel myself getting impatient, then I let that impatience decide what happens next.

A short Stoic journal gives me a place to interrupt that pattern.

## Before the day starts

In the morning, I try to write a few plain answers.

- What is in my control today?
- What may test me?
- How do I want to respond when it does?
- What is one duty I should finish well?

The questions are simple on purpose. I do not need a long entry before the day begins. I need to remember that I can control my preparation, my attention, and my response. I cannot control every request, delay, or other person's mood.

That distinction does not make the difficult parts of the day disappear. It gives me a better starting point when they arrive.

## Looking at the day honestly

At the end of the day, I write a few more lines.

- What did I do well?
- Where did I react badly or avoid my duty?
- What judgment caused that reaction?
- What will I do differently tomorrow?

The useful part is not recording events. It is noticing the story I told myself about them.

If I become irritated in a slow meeting, the problem may not be the meeting itself. I may have decided that my time is being wasted. If I avoid a difficult conversation, I may be trying to avoid discomfort rather than dealing with what needs to be said.

Writing it down makes the pattern harder to ignore. It also gives me something specific to practice the next day.

## A small way to stay on course

Stoic journaling has not made me calm all the time. That is not the goal.

It helps me create a little space between the first feeling and the next action. I still feel frustration, worry, or impatience. I just have a better chance of not letting those feelings run the day.

Some days, the entry is only a few lines. That is enough. The value is in returning to the questions and choosing, again, what kind of person I want to be in ordinary moments.
